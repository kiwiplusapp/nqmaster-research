NQMaster System Audit

Federico asked for a full audit: best practices, a cleanup of the profiles, a check of whether earlier systems tested better, more robustness against months like August 2026, and an answer on moving to a Python system with machine learning and an AI brain. Every number here comes from a script in research/mine/, run on real futures with $1.90 commission and 1 tick of slippage per side.

Did an older system test better?
No
Each version beat the one before it on the same data. Sharpe 2020-26: MaxSharpe 2.60 → Ultra 3.43.
Profiles kept
Ultra · WR70Plus · Durable · Custom
Seven removed. Durable is new: the 6 modules that held up for 16 years.
Can August be predicted?
No
Losing months behave like random draws. What breaks accounts is a few large-risk trades at a small cushion.
Main fix
Risk cap + operations layer
Funded accounts lost per year 1.65 → 0.70 (MFFU 2024-26), plus 11 code-audit fixes.
Python + AI brain?
Partly
Python as the operations and research layer: yes. A self-improving AI that trades live: no.

2 · PF and win rate

What changed inside the profiles

Each change had to hold on 2010-2019 data that was never used to choose anything (rules16_check.py, cand16.py):

  • ENG10 and LATEFH off by default. Both were rated chance-level in the overfitting audit.
  • Two context boosts removed (ORB90 after a VWAP condition, ORB60 after an ATR condition). They held in only 1 of 4 periods. Removing them cut the 2024-26 max drawdown from $5,871 to $5,221.
  • Minimum stop of 0.10 × daily ATR on structure trades (ORB, VOLB, MSEQ, CRT11, ICT, LON). Win rate rose 0.4-0.8 points in all four periods.

3 · Months like August

August cannot be predicted, but the damage can be capped

What the data says

  • 11 of 81 months lost money. Shuffling the same trades at random gives 15.6% losing months, against 13.6% in history.
  • The correlation between one month and the next is about 0. Filters on volatility, chop and VIX do not predict bad months.
  • The tail comes from 151 trades that each lost $300 or more, mostly VOLB, ORB60 and MSEQ.
  • Summer 2026: daily range 530-600 points (normal is about 340). Drawdown at 1 contract was $3,671, nearly twice the $2,000 trailing limit.

The fix: a risk cap while the cushion is small

  • While the cushion over the firm's liquidation level is below $1,200 in an eval or $2,000 in a funded account, trades risking more than $300 per contract are skipped.
  • Above those levels, every module trades as before.
  • It replaces the old SAFE mode in funded accounts.

Summer 2026 still breaks most accounts that start inside it: a $2,000 trailing limit cannot absorb a $3,700 drawdown. Two more protections were tested and rejected because they cost more than they saved:

  • A volatility guard that keeps the cap on at any cushion when the daily ATR is 1.2-1.5× its yearly median: −$150 to −$500 a month in 2020-23, with no consistent gain.
  • An intraday stop $100-400 above the liquidation level: no gain in either period.

What protects you in practice:

  • The cap.
  • Starting accounts on different weeks, so they don't all sit on a small cushion at the same time.
  • WR70Plus in funded accounts, if you value fewer busts over income.
  • The edge monitor, recalibrated to a 0.5% false-alarm rate per year for Ultra.

6 · Your question

Can all this move to a Python system with machine learning, live news and a self-improving AI brain?

Partly, and the part that matters most is the operations layer, not a new brain.

NinjaTrader executes
NQMaster and GoldMaster keep every order, stop, rule guard and position. This is the only order route both firms support. MFFU has no order API for your own code; at Lucid it would need Rithmic's conformance test plus Lucid's written approval.
Python watches
It reads the log files and a heartbeat. It sends phone alerts, reconciles each day's live fills against the research replica, tracks slippage and keeps the news calendar.
Python researches
Offline retraining on a schedule, a registry of every test, and challenger versions run in shadow mode. Nothing goes live without passing fixed gates and your approval.

Why not a self-improving AI that trades

  • Every adaptive layer tried here failed out of sample: ML meta-labeling three times, walk-forward rotation, equity-curve switching, optimised weights, context mining, order-flow sizing.
  • Telling a +0.3 Sharpe improvement from luck takes years of live data. A system that changes itself cannot know whether a change helped.
  • News: index futures price a release in about 5 milliseconds. Our own tick test lost the edge at 50 ms. At retail speed, news is a risk filter, not a source of profit.
  • LLM sentiment signals have look-ahead and memorisation bias, and their published returns decay.

Phased plan

  1. 2 weeks. Freeze v1.0, compile, run the regression backtest, start on Sim or one eval. Get the firms' answers in writing. UPS and backup line.
  2. 1-2 months, ~40-80 h. Python operations sidecar: alerts, daily reconciliation, slippage log, automatic news calendar.
  3. 2-6 months, ~80-150 h. Research platform: test registry, purged cross-validation for any ML, champion/challenger in shadow.
  4. Optional, after 6+ months live. Python execution only for your own brokerage account. You would need about $41k per base contract at a 20% volatility target, and the losses would be yours.
