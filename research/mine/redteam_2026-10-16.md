## 1. c4_cushion.py: step-up at a $4,000 cushion, keep $6,100

### (a) Simulator semantics

Source: `mffu_rapid.py` `fu_m` and `life_m`.

- **Gear and threshold.** The gear comes from the day-start cushion `bal - thr`. The threshold is `pk - 2000` until the EOD peak reaches 2,100, then it locks at +100. This matches `NQMaster.cs` `PropSessionStart` (lines 1477-1497: `propCushion = eqRef - propThr` at session start) and `StepUpOn` (line 1124).
- **Payout.** When `bal - B >= 500`, the balance is cut to B and 90% is credited as cash. After the first payout the threshold is locked at +100, so the cushion after a payout is B - 100. The logic is correct.
- **"Accounts lost"** = `nev - npass + nb`. It includes eval failures, and also the eval still running at the 12-month horizon (which is not a failure). The eval stage is identical for every policy, so this inflates all policies equally.
- **Ignored balance.** Unwithdrawn balance and the live account at the horizon end are not counted. That is conservative for the accepted policy, which parks $2,000 more.

### Bug: the haircut does not scale with size

`c4_cushion.py:33-35` `vecm` multiplies trades by `k`, but subtracts the haircut `h` (35% of the 1-contract mean daily P&L) unscaled. On 2-contract days the "35% less edge" case is really 17.5% less edge, which favours step-ups.

## 3. audit16_stats.py

### Formulas check

- **DSR** (`audit16_stats.py:45-51`) matches Bailey & López de Prado: daily SR, raw kurtosis, SR0 from the expected maximum of N normals. The trial variance is converted from annual to daily with /252. Recomputed SR0 at N = 1e5: 0.703 × 4.389 = 3.09, matching the output.
- **Binary-outcome trial SR** (`:54-62`): mean (1 - WR)(PF - 1), sd (W + 1)·sqrt(WR(1 - WR)), annualised with sqrt(n/4). This is correct. The trial SRs are measured over 4 years against a 6.8-year system, so the variance estimate is noisier, which makes SR0 conservative.
- **CSCV** (`:65-76`) is a valid variant: rank (rk + 0.5)/K, logit, PBO = P(λ ≤ 0).
- **SPA** (`:88-97`) recentres correctly with Hansen's g_c, uses a stationary bootstrap and a studentised max.

### Why the interpretation is refuted

1. **DSR treats a 20-module portfolio as one trial.**
   - Each module was itself picked from thousands of mined configs.
   - Under a portfolio-aware null, K = 20 noise modules, each the best of N/K = 5,000 trials with sd 0.70, give per-module SR ≈ 2.6 and a portfolio SR of about 2.6 × √20 ≈ 11 (equal vol, uncorrelated).
   - That is far above the observed 3.67, so DSR 0.95 cannot separate skill from selection.
   - The same test on 2010-19 cost-normalised data gives **DSR 0.000 at every N** (SR 0.85 vs SR0 2.29-3.42). This is in the json but missing from the headline.
2. **PBO 0.51 is uninformative.** The 33 variants are near-duplicates: 20 leave-one-module-out sets, 5 multiplier levels, and old profiles (2020-26 Sharpe range 3.30-3.78 apart from Durable). PBO ≈ 0.5 only says that picking among them is a coin flip. It says nothing about whether the system is overfit.
3. **SPA p≈0 is trivially guaranteed.** It tests 33 variants that are all strongly positive against zero. It does not cover the ~50k-config mining universe, which is where the data snooping happened.

- **No look-ahead.** Covariance uses `X[t-win:t]` and rebalancing is monthly.
- **Shrinkage is not the cause.** Ledoit-Wolf intensity averages 0.21; it pulls RP towards equal weights and so helps RP. Without shrinkage, RP is worse: Sharpe 2015-19 0.66 and 2020-23 3.17 (fixed: 1.21 / 3.43).
- **The 3.997 = 3.997 match in 2024-26 is real.** I recomputed it.

- **Scale-dependent criteria favour RP.** Worst month and DD are in $, and RP has smaller gross risk, so these criteria tilt *towards* RP.

## Hidden tests in this round, and multiple testing

| Source | What was evaluated |
|---|---|
| c4 | 45 policies (docstring says 48) × 2 edge levels = 90 lifecycle runs, plus 90 summer runs |
| c3 | 8 portfolio configs plus 2 redundancy switch-offs |
| audit16 | 33 variants, including 20 leave-one-out sets |
| audit16 | 20 modules × 2 eras of shrinkage decisions |
| Module-off decisions | 3 |
| Monitor | 5 sets × ~70 h grid steps |
| DSR | 5 N levels |

That is about 80 candidate changes. One was accepted (c4); the monitor recalibration is not an edge claim.

Would c4 survive a family-wise correction?

- **As a statistical discovery, no.** The paired bootstrap gives P(accepted worse in $) of 5% (2024-26) and 10% (2020-23) at full edge, and 20-23% at 35% less edge. Bonferroni over 44 alternatives needs p < 0.0011.
- **But the $ gain is a mechanical size effect, not a discovery.** The selection effect sits in the risk criterion ("losses not higher"). The bootstrap confirms that criterion out of path: funded busts are 0.46 vs 0.49 and 0.34 vs 0.35 per year.

So c4 survives as a risk-neutral size increase that pays only if the edge is real. With 35% less edge it wins in only about 50-60% of years and raises the chance of a negative year by about 6-7 points.
