# Entry-averaging lab (avg_entry.py) — 2027 default, real Databento NQ 2010-2026

Question: does averaging into entries (several entries at different prices per module) help?  Pre-registered; protocol fixed before looking at any result.

## Pre-registration

```
avg_entry: pre-registered ENTRY-AVERAGING LAB on the 2027 NQMaster default (Ultra + SO04 / RV11 / ORFAIL / CAM3 = nt_compare
'ultra_d4'), 2026-10-21.  Question (Federico): does splitting a module's position into several entries at different prices help?

Machinery: perturb_lib rebuilds every module from its ORIGINAL signal code on real Databento NQ 2010-06..2026-10 (research/data/nqdb.npz).
Per trade we take the module's entry spec (fill bar fi, side d, raw entry ent0, order type et, stop sl0, target tp0, forced time-exit
bar tx) and re-simulate the exits with a TWO-LEG version of wrq_lib.xsim (same bar order: stop first, target needs a 1-tick
trade-through and is never filled on the fill bar, time exit at the bar close - 1 tick, one position per module).  The base variant
(1 leg) reproduces the committed base trade set exactly (checked in `build`).  Costs: $1.90 commission per MNQ unit filled, 1 tick per
side on market / stop fills (limit fills pay no entry slippage), cost-normalised view = 0.345 % of daily ATR per unit, slippage 0.
Module total size = 1 base unit in every variant (0.5 + 0.5); ratio metrics are size-invariant, so the 2-lot (1 + 1) and 4-lot
(2 + 2) integer versions are the same trades x2 / x4 (reported in the json / report).

Variants (per module, ALL modules get the same geometry; k relative to the trade's own stop distance r0 = |entry - stop| or
target distance tdist = |target - entry|):
  V1 split-better  k .25/.5/.75  half at the module's entry, half as a LIMIT at entry - k x r0 (better price, i.e. an average
                                 into the pullback); same stop / target for both legs; the limit dies when the trade exits.
  V2 split-worse   k .25/.5      half at entry, half as a STOP order at entry + k x r0 (worse price; the second leg's risk is
                                 (1 + k) r0, so the total risk rises); same stop / target.
  V3 pyramid       k .25/.5 x {stop moved to the original entry for the whole position when leg 2 fills, stop unchanged}
                                 half at entry, half as a STOP order at entry + k x tdist.
  V4 all-limit     k .25/.5      100 % as a LIMIT at entry - k x r0, live until the time exit, cancelled if the target is touched
                                 first; fill rate reported (the 'limit entry' idea).
  V5 time-split    N 5/15        half at the signal, half at MARKET N bars later if the trade is still open (open not beyond the stop).
VOLB keeps its break-even rule (stop to entry + 0.1 R after +0.75 R, measured from leg 1).
Periods (Federico's split): CHOOSE on T1 = 2019-01-01..2022-12-31 (real $, days with ATR >= 150 as NQMaster's MinATR guard; and the
cost-normalised view T1n where 2019 counts in full) + A = 2010-14 + B = 2015-19 cost-normalised (regime control);
CHECK on T2 = 2023-01-01..2026-10 real $ (never used to choose).
Acceptance (fixed before looking): portfolio Sharpe (conflict filter, agreement, confluence, context rules, MinATR 150, x2 / x4
weights, all other modules unchanged) UP in T1, T1n, A and B; then T2 Sharpe not lower, T2 PF and WR not lower, T2 maxDD not
higher by more than 5 %.  Base rates P(T2 Sharpe up | T1 up) vs P(T2 up) are reported over all module x variant cells.
Global cells: every variant applied to ALL modules at once, to the structure modules only, and to the clock modules only.
Usage: python3 avg_entry.py build | run | report      (<= 2 processes; cells cached in <scratch>/avg/, resumable)
Outputs: avg_entry_cells.csv, avg_entry.json, avg_entry_report.md.  Import-safe (no work at import).
```

Interpretation note: the request's V2 'average down' reads 'entry + k x stop against the trade'. For a long, entry + k x stop is a WORSE price (above the market), so it can only be a stop order that fills once the trade is already k x stop in profit; its leg-2 risk is (1 + k) r0, which is why the request says 'total risk rises'. A true average-down (a limit between the entry and the stop) is V1. V3 differs from V2 by its distance unit (target instead of stop) and the stop-move option.

## Verdict (honest)

**Averaging into entries does not help the 2027 default.** 299 module x variant cells and 39 global cells were run; 11 cells raise the portfolio Sharpe in all four choose periods (T1 real, T1 cost-normalised, 2010-14, 2015-19) and 1 of them also passes the full T2 check.
- The formal survivor **MSEQ V5_N15** is a noise-level cell: portfolio Sharpe deltas T1 +0.003, T1n +0.030, A +0.033, B +0.004, T2 +0.000 (it passes 'T2 not lower' by equality at 3 decimals); T2 net $98,889 vs base $99,260 and T1 net $78,509 vs $78,898 (both LOWER), T2 maxDD 4,680 vs 4,668. It is not worth two lots per MSEQ trade and a second order; NOT recommended for NQMaster.
- Selection bias: P(T2 Sharpe up | T1 up) = 0.24 vs P(T2 up) overall = 0.25; choosing on T1 has no predictive value here (among the 11 choose-set passes, T2 Sharpe rises in 0.36 and the full T2 check holds in 0.09).
- Mechanism (see the standalone tables): V1 (limit for the second half at a better price) raises WR by 1-3 points but the second leg fills on 35-70 % of trades and fills more often on losers (the pullback that fills the limit is the start of the stop-out) -> net $ down 10-45 % with the same risk per trade; V2 / V3 (adding on a stop order once in profit) lowers WR 1-2 points (clock) / 3-5 (structure) and net $ 16-55 %; moving the stop to the original entry on a pyramid (V3 sm1) is catastrophic (WR -16..-39 points: the add turns a normal winner into a scratch); V4 (all-limit) loses 25-58 % of the signals (adverse selection, worse on clock modules) and is the worst family on 2010-19; V5 (half now, half 5-15 min later) is the closest to neutral in ratios (fill 80-95 %, WR +-1) but still loses 5-20 % of net $ (second slippage, later entry).
- Global: no scope x variant passes the choose set. 'All modules V1 k0.25' has the best T2 ratios (Sharpe 3.68 vs 3.60, PF 1.47 vs 1.44, WR 66.6 vs 64.5, maxDD 4.1k vs 4.7k) but net $ -12 % with 0.82-0.86 average size, and it FAILS 2010-14 / 2015-19 (Sharpe 0.25 vs 0.62, 1.03 vs 1.31): the ratio gain is a 2019-26 artefact of smaller size on the runaway winners, not an entry edge.
- Integer lots: every split needs at least 2 MNQ per module (2 x the account exposure of the current 1-lot setup) and earns less than the plain 2-lot base; the 1-contract prop plan cannot use any of these variants. Earlier partial results (gerard.py scale-in on the 5-min ORB, Tradefather scale-in, runners, limit entries on clock modules) are confirmed system-wide.
- NQMaster implementation: NONE recommended. For the record, the formal survivor would be: MSEQ EnterLong(1 lot) at the signal as today, then 15 one-minute bars later, if the position is still open (not stopped, not at target), EnterLong(1 lot) at market (second signal name 'MSEQ-2'), both lots sharing the same stop / target prices (SetStopLoss / SetProfitTarget per signal name at the ORIGINAL prices, not re-centred on the average); the time exit flattens both. Expected effect: $0 +- noise.
- Do not re-run with other k / N: V1 k .25/.5/.75, V2 k .25/.5, V3 k .25/.5 x stop-move, V4 k .25/.5, V5 N 5/15 cover the plateau and every family degrades monotonically with k; a new ingredient (e.g. order-flow-conditioned adds) would be needed, and order-flow filters already failed out of sample (2026-10-09).
